熊野

熊野Kumano. The mountain province where the founder was born, and to which he returned all his life.

The founder was born in Kumano.

Morihei Ueshiba, the founder of aikido, was born in Tanabe on the Kumano coast in 1883. The pilgrim roads that cross those mountains, the Kumano Kodō, have been walked for a thousand years; the cedar steps at Daimon-zaka still climb to the Nachi falls. He shaped the art far from here, over forty years, but he returned to these mountains all his life, and the art kept their temper: nothing is forced, everything is joined.

Aikido West carries that art, unchanged in principle, to a hall off Brook Green. The line from the founder to this mat has four names and no gaps.

結びmusubi. A tying together: two people in one movement, and one teacher to the next.

Kazuo Chiba brought the art to Britain, 1966

Minoru Kanetsuka Hombu's representative here for four decades

Arabella Braun his student from 1988, Brook Green

Kanetsuka Sensei met Chiba Sensei in Kathmandu in 1972 and came to England at his invitation. When Chiba Sensei returned to Japan, Kanetsuka Sensei succeeded him: Technical Director of the British Aikido Federation from 1978, and the Aikikai Hombu Dojo's representative in this country until his death in 2019. Arabella trained under him from 1988 to the end. What she teaches is what he taught.

Read the line
墨

One movement, finished.

Two people join into one movement, and the movement ends where it joins. Nothing is forced, nothing is left over. It is the quietest of the martial arts, and the hardest to hurry.

Sumi in water. The ink leaves the trace; the water is still again.

畳

稽古keiko. Practice; in the old characters, to consider what came before.

A hall off Brook Green. Saturday, half past nine.

Aikido West trains at the Masbro Centre, a community hall between Brook Green and Olympia, on Saturday mornings and Tuesday evenings. Classes are two hours, taught at the pace of the slowest person on the mat, and small enough that nobody trains at the back. Arabella trains with everyone in the room, every class. A beginner's first lesson is how to fall.

The dojo is affiliated to the British Aikido Federation and recognised by the Aikikai Foundation, Hombu Dojo, Tokyo. The grades given here are the grades given in Japan.

手

A correction is a kindness.

Most of what sensei teaches is said with two fingers on an elbow. Thirty one years under Kanetsuka Sensei taught her that the quiet correction outlasts the loud one, and that a student should be able to train into old age.

The beginner and the senior grade are held by the same hands. That is the whole method. It does not scale, and it is not meant to.

庭

Kyoto, fifteen minutes on foot.

The Kyoto Garden in Holland Park was given to London by the city of Kyoto in 1991. It is the nearest thing to the art's own landscape, and it is a short walk from the mat.
